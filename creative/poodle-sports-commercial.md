# SMALL DOG. BIG ENERGY.

**A 10.000-second motion-graphics commercial — production package for Google Flow (Omni Flash).**

Fictional premium sports brand. Original graphic world. No existing brand identity, logo, slogan, or trademarked design language is referenced or implied.

- **Duration:** 10.000 s
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps (240 frames total; all frame counts below assume 24 fps)
- **Aspect:** 16:9 primary, 9:16 alt framing noted in §7
- **Discipline:** bold minimalist 2D flat-vector motion design, no photorealism

---

## 1. Style bible

**Look:** crisp flat-vector 2D. Every form is a solid shape with a hard edge. No gradients dominating any frame, no texture, no rendered fur, no 3D lighting, no lens artifacts. Line weights are consistent and geometric. Composition is high-contrast editorial: enormous negative space, then sudden full-frame takeover.

**Character:** a tiny brown poodle, built from simple geometry — round head, two floppy ear curves, two dot eyes, one small oval nose, compact athletic body, a single arc for the tail, four short legs. Fluffy silhouette is suggested by scalloped edge shapes only, never by rendered hair. Adorable proportions, world-class-athlete intensity. The joke is the contrast and it is never winked at.

**Motion language:** stillness → explosion. The film alternates hard between dead-still holds and violent bursts. Squash and stretch is aggressive (up to 180% stretch, 155% squash). Anticipation before every launch. 6–8% overshoot with a fast settle on every landing. Follow-through on ears and tail always. Silhouette continuity carries every transition — graphic match cuts only, **never a crossfade, never a dissolve, never a soft blend.**

**Scale rhetoric:** the poodle is tiny in the frame at rest and takes over the entire frame at impact. Small → massive → small.

## 4. Shot-by-shot

### SHOT 1 — LOCK IN · 00:00.000–00:01.200 · frames 0–28

Warm cream fills the frame. A single near-black horizontal stroke sits on the lower third: the starting line. The poodle stands in profile facing right, tiny, alone, occupying maybe 12% of the frame height. Vast empty cream above it.

Dead still. Four frames of nothing.

**f6** — one ear twitches, a 9° rotation and snap back over three frames. **CLICK** (dry, tiny, isolated).

**f10–f18** — the poodle lowers its front body into a ready stance, chest dropping, shoulders compressing. The tail lifts and holds at a hard diagonal. No easing-out softness — it arrives and stops.

**f14** — a thin acid-lime line appears under the paws and charges left-to-right like a pressure sensor filling. Two frames to draw, six to fill.

**f24** — the lime bar completes. **THUMP** (deep miniature bass).

**f25–f26** — absolute hold. Two frames. Nothing moves. This is the loudest silence in the film.

**f27** — launch begins.

*Sound:* micro-silence, then CLICK on the ear, a low charging hum under the lime bar, THUMP at full charge, then silence for the two-frame hold.

---

### SHOT 2 — SPRINT BURST · 00:01.200–00:02.800 · frames 29–67

**f29** — the poodle explodes forward. **WHIP + BOOM.** Its body stretches to 180% of resting length for four frames, ears streaming straight back like racing flags, tail a single trailing arc.

**f33 onward** — footfall rhythm: **TOK — TOK — TOK — BOOM**, one hit every three frames. Each footfall punches a bold geometric impact shape into the cream behind it, cycling circle → triangle → square → arc in near-black and cobalt. Each shape lives two to four frames and vanishes — cut out, not faded.

**f45–f55** — as speed peaks the silhouette morphs through shared outlines, hard cut on every swap, four frames apart:

> poodle → speed arrow → running streak → poodle

No crossfade. Each shape shares the previous one's outer contour at the cut frame so the eye reads one continuous body.

**f52–f53** — the entire background flips to electric cobalt for two frames. The poodle reads as cream against it. Then it snaps back to warm cream.

**f58–f64** — the poodle skids sideways, body angled hard, legs braced, ears and tail overshooting forward past the body and settling back.

**f64** — a giant acid-lime streak cuts across the whole frame on a single frame, edge to edge, and wipes the shot.

*Sound:* WHIP on launch layered with a deep BOOM. TOK TOK TOK on the footfalls, locked to the woodblock. A rising air swish into the skid. Hard SWISH on the lime streak.

### SHOT 3 — EVERYDAY OBJECTS AS TRAINING COURSE · 00:02.800–00:04.800 · frames 67–115

Ordinary dog-life objects treated as elite athletic apparatus. Every object is a clean flat graphic symbol — a circle, a ring, a bar, a curve — never an illustration of the real thing.

| Beat | Object | Athletic role | Action | Frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | tennis ball | hoop | jumps through the ring | f67–f77 |
| 2 | hoop | hurdle | clears the bar | f77–f87 |
| 3 | sofa edge | ramp | runs up the incline | f87–f97 |
| 4 | food bowl | circular target | spins around the rim | f97–f107 |
| 5 | leash loop | tunnel | dives through | f107–f115 |

**Every transition is a geometric morph, not a cut to a new object:**

- the tennis-ball circle **opens** into the hoop ring
- the hoop ring **flattens** into the hurdle bar
- the bar **bends** into the ramp
- the ramp **curves** into the bowl
- the bowl loop **unrolls** into the leash tunnel

Each morph takes three to four frames and the poodle never breaks stride across it. The background stays warm cream; objects are cobalt and near-black; the lime appears only as the contact flash at each touch point.

*Sound, synchronised to contact:* **POP — SNAP — SWISH — TOK — BOOM**, one per obstacle, landing on the beat.

**f103** — music intensity steps up a level.

**f110 (00:04.300)** — the poodle launches off the final ramp, body stretching into the takeoff.

---

### SHOT 4 — AIRTIME AND IMPACT · 00:04.800–00:06.300 · frames 115–151

**f115–f118** — the frame goes full acid lime. The poodle is frozen midair, cocoa brown against the lime, dead centre-high in the composition. Four frames of total motion arrest.

**f117** — it blinks once. Eyes close and open over two frames. **CLICK** — one isolated sound, nothing else in the mix.

**f119** — motion resumes explosively. The poodle spins through the air, morphing every four to five frames, each swap a hard silhouette cut:

> poodle → paw print → heart → lightning bolt → poodle

Each shape is crisp, flat, and reads instantly in silhouette. Background returns to warm cream at **f121**.

**f135–f143** — the dive. The poodle drops, accelerating, body compressing into a vertical form.

**f144** — at the frame before impact it shrinks to a single tiny dot. **Hold two frames.** Silence.

**f146** — **MASSIVE LANDING. BOOOOM.** The poodle squashes to 155% width, flattened against the landing line, then rebounds with overshoot over the next four frames.

**f146–f151** — three concentric shock rings fire outward in sequence: coral red first, then electric cobalt, then acid lime. Each ring is a hard flat stroke, expanding to fill and then exceed the frame, wiping the visual space clean for the next shot.

*Sound:* full mix dropout at f115. Isolated CLICK at the blink. Rising suction fwoomp on the dive. Deep miniature bass BOOM at impact, layered with three ring transients.

### SHOT 5 — MOTION BECOMES TYPOGRAPHY · 00:06.300–00:08.500 · frames 151–204

The text is never revealed — it is **built**. Every letter is visibly constructed from a shape the film has already used. The audience should recognise the parts.

**Two lines, bold geometric uppercase, near-black on warm cream:**

```
SMALL DOG.
BIG ENERGY.
```

**Letter sourcing — each element flies in from the direction it last exited:**

| Letter | Built from |
|---|---|
| **S** | the tail arc, doubled and reversed |
| **M** | two ramp strokes mirrored |
| **A** | a paw shape, splayed and squared |
| **L L** | two long speed lines, dropped vertical |
| **D** | a hoop ring, halved and set on a speed line |
| **O** | an impact circle |
| **G** | an ear curve closed with a short bar |
| **B** | two shock-ring fragments stacked |
| **I** | a single footfall speed line |
| **N** | a ramp stroke with two verticals |
| **E** | three speed lines on a vertical |
| **R** | a hoop fragment on a leg |
| **Y** | the lightning bolt, straightened |
| **C** | an open shock ring |

**Landing rhythm** — letters do not arrive together. They land in staggered groups of two or three, roughly three frames apart, each with a hard stop and 6–8% overshoot settling in two frames. The phrase assembles with the feeling of momentum being spent, not of text appearing.

*Sound:* **CLACK — TOK — SNAP — BOOM**, one transient per letter group, locked to the percussion. The groove is at maximum intensity here.

**f197–f200** — on the final word landing, the colours invert for four frames: full electric cobalt background, warm cream typography, one acid-lime accent shape. Then it snaps back — hard cut — to warm cream with near-black type.

**f200–f204** — the phrase holds, locked and still.

### SHOT 6 — FINAL HERO POSE · 00:08.500–00:10.000 · frames 204–240

**f204–f208** — hold the full phrase. 0.15 s of stillness. Instruments drop out of the mix.

**f208** — the period after **DOG** suddenly pops free of the baseline and becomes a small ball, coral red. **POP** (elastic).

**f209–f222** — the poodle enters instantly from frame left and chases the ball across the front of the typography, passing *in front of* the letters, trailing a continuous coral-red ribbon behind it. The ribbon is a single unbroken stroke that follows the exact path of the run.

**f222–f226** — the ribbon snaps into a clean straight underline beneath the full phrase. **SNAP** (magnetic).

**f226–f230** — the poodle catches the ball, lands centre-front below the type, and strikes the hero pose: one paw slightly forward, chest out, tail up, confident expression. Overshoot and settle in three frames.

**f230** — one tiny head tilt, 7°. **CLICK** (dry, isolated, the last sound in the film).

**f230** — a simple circular paw-mark symbol appears beside the poodle — a flat cocoa-brown circle containing one pad shape and four toe dots. An original fictional brand mark. It does not animate; it is simply there on the frame it arrives.

**f231 (00:09.650)** — **freeze everything.** Absolute lock.

**f231–f240** — hold the frozen frame through 00:10.000. Silence.

## 9. Music

Aggressive minimalist electronic-percussion groove, **160 BPM** (0.375 s per beat; 26.6 beats across the film — the shot boundaries fall close to beats 3, 7, 13, 17, 23).

**Instrumentation:** hard muted kick, dry clap, tight electronic percussion, short bass stabs, minimal synth pulse, small woodblock rhythm, occasional low impact hit.

**Arrangement:**

- **00:00.000–00:01.200** — near silence. A single synth pulse under the charge. Micro-silence at the two-frame hold.
- **00:01.200–00:02.500** — kick and woodblock enter on the launch, locked to the footfall rhythm.
- **00:02.500–00:08.200** — continuous build. Clap enters at 00:02.800, bass stabs at 00:04.300, full percussion stack from 00:06.300. Hard dropouts at 00:04.800 and 00:06.000 for contrast — the silences carry the impacts.
- **00:08.200–00:08.500** — drop most instruments. Only a low sustain remains.
- **00:08.500–00:09.650** — sparse. Woodblock only under the ball chase.
- **00:09.650–00:10.000** — one isolated CLICK, then nothing.

## 10. Motion principles

Explosive acceleration. Hard stop-and-go rhythm. Squash and stretch. Anticipation before every move. Overshoot on every landing. Follow-through on ears and tail. Silhouette continuity through every morph. Graphic match cuts. Clear cause and effect — every shape that appears was caused by something. Athletic impact weight. Precise registration on the grid. Cute but powerful character acting.

Every motion is intentionally designed and physically motivated. Nothing drifts, nothing eases in softly, nothing happens because it looks nice.
